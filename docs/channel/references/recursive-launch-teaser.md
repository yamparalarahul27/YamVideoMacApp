# Reference: Recursive launch teaser

**Saved:** 26 Oct 2026, to use as a style guide for upcoming videos.
**Source:** a 34.6 s clip the owner "can't stop rewatching" (shared by Mark Vassilevskiy),
1920×1080, 30 fps, stereo.
**What it is:** Richard Socher, co-founder and CEO of Recursive, launches their AI model
Flint and the "Eureka Machine" that built it. One speaker talks to the camera, and almost
every sentence gets a graphic.
**Video and pictures:** on the Yamparala Mac, in `~/Desktop/Yamparala Media/Shared
Assets/References/Recursive launch teaser/`: the video, `Contact sheet.png` (one frame a
second) and `Frames/` (one frame for each beat, named by time). Git keeps only these
notes: no frame or video of the reference is in this repository.

A reference is for study only. No frame, logo, chart, music or words from it go into one
of our videos. These notes are not Style.md rules: a rule from here goes into Style.md
only after the owner says yes.

## In one line

Every sentence gets one picture that shows what it says, in one look (one dark page, one
light page, one accent, one grid), and the speaker stands inside the graphics, not under
them.

## The story

The order of ideas is what keeps you watching:

**who → why trust me → claim → proof → surprise → question → answer → number → contrast → logo**

It ends on a cliffhanger: the last line sets up a contrast ("most labs hire thousands of
researchers") and cuts to the logo before the answer.

## Edit rhythm

- A new picture about every 2–3 s (13 beats in 34.6 s), and something moves inside each
  one. The longest single picture is the paths (about 5 s), and it keeps growing.
- Two framings of the same take: a wide (head to waist, speaker in the centre) and a tight
  close-up (face on the left third). The wide is home; the close-up is for the personal
  lines (his role, the question).
- Speaker on screen about 40% of the time, full-screen graphics about 60%.
- Every change lands on a sentence start. Hard cuts between speaker and graphics; pushes
  and zooms inside a run of graphics.

## Look

**Colours** (sampled from the frames):

| Use | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| Dark page | aubergine near-black, about `#211723`, with a fine grid one step lighter |
| Light page | near-white, about `#FDFDFD`, with thin dashed grey guide lines |
| Accent | warm orange, about `#EC8352`. Bars fade from about `#F2A87C` at the top to pale lilac at the bottom, with an orange glow at the floor |
| Text, rival bars | white |

The accent means one thing only: "ours" (their model's bar, the active parameters, a few
dots in the paths). Everything else is white or grey.

**Grid.** Thin dashed guide lines, like a design tool's layout guides, are on every page and
faintly over the footage too. They tie the speaker shots and the graphics into one world.

**Type.** One clean sans (Inter or SF Pro type), regular weight, white, sentence case,
tight line spacing. Big: in speaker shots the capitals are about 10% of the frame height
(about 100 px tall at 1080p, a font size of about 130–140 px). Lines are left-aligned and
sometimes stepped (each line starts a little further right). No box or shadow under the
text: it sits straight on the soft-focus wall. Small labels (chart titles, "40B
Parameters") are about 2.5–3% of the height. Word chips are black text on small white
rounded boxes.

**Layers.** The name, the year bars, the "4 M" pixels and the dot wall sit between the wall
and the speaker: his body covers them. This depth is the most high-end look in the clip,
and it needs a cut-out of the speaker on every frame. Some text ("Co-created") sits in
front of him instead.

**Footage.** Cameras locked off, shallow depth of field, a calm warm wall with sun spots and
a plant, a plain black T-shirt with the brand mark. The background is quiet so the graphics
can live on it.

## Motion

- Text slides in sideways in about 0.3 s (fast start, soft stop), often coming out from
  behind the speaker. One line or one word at a time, on the frame it is said.
- Numbers count up while bars grow (about 1 s).
- Graphics grow from one thing: one dot → many paths; a few squares → a full grid; a few
  dots → a full wall.
- Inside a run of graphics: sideways pushes (the page moves left, the next comes in from
  the right) and slow push-ins.
- Logo: a blur-in with a small scale, then a hold of about 1.5 s.
- Nothing bounces or spins. The moves are smooth and calm; the energy comes from how
  often the picture changes.

## Sound

From a level trace of the file, not listened to.

- Voice dry, close and in front.
- A low music bed under the whole clip. In pauses the bass stays at about −31 dB, about
  10–15 dB under the voice. A short near-silent dip at about 3.5 s, just before the cut to
  the wide shot.
- The bed fades out over the logo, from about 32.5 s to the end.

## Using it in our videos

What to take (keep our own accent `#F5C42C` and the phones-first rule from Style.md):

1. **One picture per sentence** for the hook of long videos (the first 30 s) and for
   launch Shorts. Write the beat table first: each line, its picture, its move.
2. **The story order** for a launch or a big claim: who → why trust me → claim → proof →
   surprise → question → answer → number → contrast → end card.
3. **One look per video:** one dark page, one light page, one accent that only means
   "mine".
4. **The guide-line grid** as the link between speaker shots and graphics.
5. **Big plain type straight on the footage**, word by word, no box, where the background
   is soft. Check at phone size first.
6. **Turn every number into a picture made of it:** a number built from pixels, one dot
   per person, coloured squares for "the part that counts".
7. **Two framings from one camera:** the Pocket 3 close-up and a 1.15x push-in, switched
   only at sentence starts (as in the System design series rule).
8. **End on a cliffhanger** before the end card when it is a teaser.

For Shorts (9:16): the same beats work. Keep text in the clear area and off the face, as
the Shorts rules in Style.md say; graphics pages fill the whole frame.

What YamVideo would need for some of this (from a quick search of main at PR #87):

- Text and graphics **behind the speaker** need a person cut-out on video. Today only
  Thumbnail Check uses a person mask.
- **Counters, growing bars, a pixels-to-number reveal and a dot wall** would be new
  graphics.
